00 · USER MANUAL — HOW TO SIZE A PUMP ▼

Work down the sections in order. Each one feeds the next, and the panel recalculates as you type — you do not have to finish before you see results. Nothing here needs a vendor quotation to get started.

Starting a new pump? Press RESET first. The panel keeps the previous project's inputs (and its Suction Source Type, machine data and results) sitting in every field until you clear them — beginning a fresh design on top of an old one is how numbers from two different pumps end up mixed together. RESET returns every field, chart and report to its untouched starting state before you enter a single value.
Set your units first. The unit system selector in the top bar drives the whole suite — SI (m, bar, kg/hr), US customary (ft, psi, GPM, HP) or mixed metric (cm, kg/cm², L/min, g/s). You may switch at any time, including after a run: every input, output, chart, schematic and report converts together. Switching units never changes the answer, only how it is written.

Step 1 — 01 · Design data sheet

Company, project location, pump tag number, service description, engineer, date and revision. None of it affects the calculation; all of it is printed on the report and the schematic, so fill it in if the output is going into a document package.

Step 2 — 02 · Fluid properties

  1. Service Fluid — pick from the list. Density, viscosity and the vapour-pressure basis are filled in for you.
  2. Liquid Density and Dynamic Viscosity — overwrite the filled values whenever you have datasheet figures. Viscosity above roughly 20 cSt triggers the ANSI/HI 9.6.7 viscous correction automatically.
  3. Vapor Pressure — calculated from the operating temperature by the Antoine equation, with constants from Perry's Chemical Engineers' Handbook, Table 2-8. It is editable: type over it if you have a measured figure. Choosing user defined as the fluid leaves it blank for you to fill in.
Petroleum cuts (crude, diesel, condensate, LPG, fuel oil) are evaluated through a named pseudo-component of similar volatility, and the substitution is stated under the field. A real cut's vapour pressure is set by its light ends — confirm against the assay RVP before you commit the NPSH margin.

Step 3 — 03 · Operating temperature

One temperature drives the vapour pressure, and through it the NPSH available. The vapour pressure vs temperature graph beside it is drawn for the fluid you selected, with your operating point marked. If the temperature falls outside the range the Antoine constants were fitted over, the figure is still given but flagged as extrapolated.

Step 4 — 04 · Process sizing flowrates

Enter Normal Vol Flow only. Mass flow and volumetric flow are derived from it and the density. Design Flow Margin is a percentage added on top; the Design Flowrate that results is the flow every downstream calculation uses.

Step 5 — 05 · Suction line & elevation

  1. Suction Source Type — atmospheric or pressurised. Atmospheric ignores the vessel pressure you type and uses ambient.
  2. Vessel Pressure — gauge. The absolute equivalent is shown beside it.
  3. Plant Grade Elevation is the datum, normally zero. Vessel Elevation is the height of the vessel base above it, and Pump CL Elevation the pump centreline.
  4. LLL is entered as a percentage of vessel height, not as an elevation. The static head the pump sees is the resulting liquid level minus the pump centreline — negative when the pump sits above the level, which is a suction lift.
  5. Suction Line ΔP Reference — see the box below.
Choosing the line loss. The ΔP table is one choice, made once:
  • Short / normal / long run — a pipe-layout approximation. Use it for early work when the routing is not drawn yet.
  • User defined — a figure you already have from a line-sizing study.
  • Calculated from the piping — opens the calculator below the table. Enter NPS, schedule, run length and fitting counts; the loss is worked out by Darcy–Weisbach with Colebrook friction over ASME B36.10M bores and Crane TP‑410 fitting K values.
The calculator always shows its result, and states whether that result is the one in force. Only the row you select governs.

Step 6 — 06 · Discharge side

Max Destination Pressure (absolute) and Discharge Elevation set where the liquid has to arrive. The discharge ΔP works exactly as the suction side, with its own calculator. Shut‑off Head Margin sets the head rise used when checking the curve to shut‑off.

Elevations are measured against plant grade, but the lift the pump does is measured from its own centreline — raising the pump reduces the differential head, it does not increase it.

Step 7 — 07 · Power calculations configuration

Two reference tables, one for pump efficiency and one for motor efficiency. A band is highlighted automatically to match the calculated power. Select a row yourself to override it — your choice is then held and is not overwritten on the next recalculation. The AUTO button beside each table hands the choice back to the software. Motor Service Factor is applied when sizing the motor.

Step 8 — 08 · Machine data (standards checks)

Optional, and only used for cross-checking against the standards.

  • Pump Speed, Stages, Impeller Suction (single or double) — feed specific speed and suction specific speed.
  • Design Suction Sp. Speed (Nss) — the eye the pump would be built to. 9000 is a conventional process pump; a conservative eye is nearer 7000–8500.
  • Rated Flow as % of BEP — where your duty sits on the machine. API 610 §6.1.4 wants the rated point between 80 % and 110 % of best efficiency.
  • Predict the pump curve — when ticked, and when you have not entered an NPSHr, the software models head, efficiency, NPSHr and power against flow, and finds the operating point where the pump curve crosses the system curve.

Step 9 — 09 · Nozzle selection

Both nozzles default to AUTO, which selects the smallest standard bore meeting the target velocity (2.0 m/s suction, 3.5 m/s discharge by default — both editable in section 08). Nozzle sizes are input data, so pick them yourself when the pump is already chosen; the software then reports the velocity you would actually get and whether it is acceptable, alongside what it would have selected.

Step 10 — Run

The amber REQUIRED INPUTS panel above the run button lists the eleven compulsory entries and strikes each one through as you supply it. Press RUN PUMP CALCULATION when it is clear.

Reading the results

  • NPSH cavitation margin — the gauge shows NPSHa against NPSHr with the margin marked. Its scale is set by the duty, so read the end labels rather than assuming a fixed span.
  • Pump curve chart — head, system head, NPSHr and efficiency against flow, with the API 610 preferred operating region (70–120 % of BEP) shaded and the operating point marked.
  • Standards checks — each one names the clause it applies (API 610, ANSI/HI, ASME, IEC) and reports pass, review or fail.
  • Schematic and 3D view — drawn from your elevations and pressures, and updated as you type.

Auto‑design

When a check fails, the design assistant lists the correction with an APPLY button that writes the value into the input it belongs to. When nothing fails you get a green “DESIGN IS SOUND — NO AUTO‑DESIGN CORRECTION NEEDED” banner naming every margin that was checked and the value it passed on, so a clear design is as obvious as a failing one.

Report

Download the PDF from the report button. It carries the design data sheet, all inputs and outputs, the schematic, the standards table and the performance graphs, in whichever unit system is active.

What is predicted and what is not. Where you have not supplied a figure, NPSHr and efficiency are predicted from suction specific speed and Hydraulic Institute attainable-efficiency data, and the whole pump curve is a model, not a rating. The panel labels every predicted figure as predicted. They exist so a duty can be placed on a machine and checked before an enquiry goes out — replace them with the vendor curve before purchase.

00 · USER MANUAL — HOW TO SIZE A DOUBLE PIPE HEAT EXCHANGER ▼

A double pipe (hairpin) exchanger is one tube inside one shell, in series hairpins; it suits duties too small to justify a shell & tube bundle, or a high-fouling stream that needs to be pulled apart for cleaning often.

Starting a new exchanger? Press RESET first. The panel keeps the previous design's inputs — fluids, geometry, fouling, results and 3D view — sitting in every field until you clear them. RESET returns every field, chart, diagram and report to its untouched starting state before you enter a single value.
Set your units first. The unit system selector in the top bar drives this exchanger and the whole suite together — SI (m, bar, kg/hr), US customary (ft, psi, GPM, °F) or mixed metric (cm, kg/cm², L/min, g/s). You may switch at any time, including after a run: every input, output, chart, 3D diagram and report converts together. Switching units never changes the answer, only how it is written — the calculation itself always runs in SI underneath.

Step 1 — A · Process parameters

  1. Hot Fluid Placement — a design choice, not a fixed rule. Thumb rule: put a high-pressure, corrosive or fouling stream (and cooling water) in the inner tube, since only that pipe then needs the expensive alloy and is the one you pull for cleaning; put a high-viscosity or high-volume stream in the annulus, which has the larger flow area and so the lower pressure drop.
  2. Fluid (Hot Stream) / (Cold Stream) — pick from the list to fill density, viscosity, specific heat and thermal conductivity for you, or leave on User Defined and type your own datasheet figures directly into the property fields below.
  3. Inner / Outer Pipe Material — sets the wall conductivity used in the U calculation; each side is independent, since the inner pipe and the annulus pipe are frequently different alloys.
  4. Phase In/Out — optional, for your own record and the report; the thermal calculation itself is single-phase sensible heat.
  5. Smart Input — pick the one quantity the software should calculate from the energy balance Q = m·Cp·ΔT; the other five (both flows, both inlet temps, the two outlet temps) are then your inputs. Leaving it on Calculate HOT Outlet Temp is the usual starting point when you know both flows and the cold side's target rise.
  6. Flow Rate, Inlet/Outlet Temp, Cp, k, Density, Viscosity — whichever of these the Smart Input mode has not marked AUTO-CALCULATED are your inputs; the auto field is greyed and filled in for you once you run the calculation.

Step 2 — B · Geometry & dimensions

  1. Inner Pipe (NPS) and Outer Pipe (NPS) — pick standard ASME B36.10 bores, or use the Quick Config preset for a common inner/outer pairing. Selecting either fills the ID/OD/D2 fields below; you may still overwrite those fields directly if your actual pipe differs from the standard table.
  2. Standard Pipe Length and Number of Hairpins — set the length of one hairpin leg and how many are connected in series. Pipe Length per Hairpin (L) and Number of Hairpins (n) below mirror the selects and are editable directly for a non-standard length.

Step 3 — C · Fouling & wall

Fouling Inner (Rdi) and Fouling Outer (Rdo) set the resistance allowance for scale and deposit build-up on each side over the exchanger's service life — leave the TEMA-typical defaults unless you have a fluid-specific figure. Wall Conductivity (kw) is filled in automatically from the inner pipe material chosen in Step 1, and is editable if you need a temperature-corrected value.

Step 4 — Run

Press RUN DPHE CALCULATION. The 3D double-pipe view, the results panel and every chart populate together.

Reading the results

  • Thermal Duty — heat duty Q, the corrected LMTD, and the outlet temperature the Smart Input mode solved for.
  • Heat Transfer Coefficients — the inner-pipe film coefficient corrected to the outside diameter (hio), the annulus film coefficient (ho), and the overall U both dirty (with fouling) and clean.
  • Area & Hairpins — area required against area available at the hairpin count you set, and the % Excess Area that tells you whether the design is tight, comfortable or oversized.
  • Tube Side / Annulus Side — Detailed — Reynolds, Prandtl and Nusselt numbers, friction factor, pressure drop and the recommended nozzle bore for each side, so a failing ΔP or a laminar Reynolds number can be traced to its side directly.
  • Key Results panel — every important output tagged AUTO-CALCULATED or USER INPUT, so it is always clear which numbers the software solved for and which you supplied.
  • U₀ Verification & Operating Envelope charts — the assumed U against the calculated U for both sides, the safety margin across 50–150% of design flow, the estimated U₀ band for the service-fluid category, and a phase-change behaviour profile inferred from the fluids and phases chosen.

Auto-upgrade & flow comparison

When a check is outside its band, AUTO-UPGRADE SUGGESTIONS lists the correction with its reasoning; APPLY RECOMMENDED VALUES writes every suggestion into its input in one step. FLOW COMPARISON runs the same duty as counter-current and concurrent side by side, so you can see the LMTD and effectiveness penalty of a co-current arrangement before committing to the piping layout.

Report & manufacturing drawing

DOWNLOAD DPHE REPORT produces the PDF datasheet — inputs, results, both sides' detailed hydraulics and the verification charts, in whichever unit system is active. 2D MFG DRAWING + BOM opens the fabrication drawing: hairpin layout, nozzle schedule and bill of materials, dimensioned in the same active unit system.

Check the outlet temperature after Smart Input changes. Switching which variable is calculated, or re-running with a stale outlet temperature already sitting in a field from an earlier partial run, can leave an inconsistent pair of hot/cold temperatures in place for one recalculation. If a result looks physically wrong (an inlet colder than its own outlet on the same side), re-enter the temperatures for that run and recalculate rather than trusting the carried-over value.

📋 DESIGN SEQUENCE GUIDE — PARAMETER SELECTION ORDER
STEP 1: TUBE OD (Do) -
Recommended: 19mm (3/4") for general service. 25mm for viscous/fouling fluids. 12.7mm for high pressure/compact design.
STEP 2: TUBE ID (Di) & WALL THICKNESS -
Di = Do - 2 × wall_thickness. Recommended Di = Do - 3mm. Affects tube velocity and Re.
STEP 3: TUBE LAYOUT -
Triangular: 15-30% more tubes, higher HTC (clean fluids). Square: easier cleaning (fouling fluids).
STEP 4: PITCH RATIO (Pt/Do) -
TEMA minimum: 1.25 × Do. Higher pitch = lower pressure drop but lower heat transfer.
STEP 5: TUBE LENGTH (L) -
Standard lengths: 3000, 4000, 6000, 7270mm. L/Ds ratio = 5-10 for good design.
STEP 6: TUBE PASSES (Np) -
Higher Np = higher tube velocity & HTC, but higher pressure drop. Even numbers only.
STEP 7: BAFFLE SPACING (B) -
Rule: 0.2×Ds ≤ B ≤ Ds. Optimum: 0.3-0.5 × Ds for best HTC/ΔP ratio.
STEP 8: SHELL ID (Ds) -
Always round UP to nearest standard TEMA shell ID step.
00 · USER MANUAL — HOW TO SIZE A SHELL & TUBE HEAT EXCHANGER ▼

Work down the sections in order — the panel recalculates as you type, so you see results before you finish, and can correct a section without losing anything already entered. This module sizes to the Kern method with TEMA geometry rules, and lets you fix the design to a real TEMA head/shell/rear combination in one click.

Set your units first. The unit system selector in the top bar drives this exchanger and the whole suite together — SI (m, bar, kg/hr), US customary (ft, psi, GPM, °F) or mixed metric (cm, kg/cm², L/min, g/s). You may switch at any time, including after a run: every input, output, chart, TEMA drawing and report converts together. Switching units never changes the answer, only how it is written — the calculation itself always runs in SI underneath.

Step 1 — Fluid allocation configuration

  1. Tube Side Fluid and Shell Side Fluid — pick from the list to fill density, viscosity, specific heat, thermal conductivity and a typical operating pressure for you, or leave on User Defined and enter your own datasheet figures directly.
  2. Tube Side Phase / Shell Side Phase — Liquid/Liquid, Liquid/Vapor (boiling), Vapor/Liquid (condensing) or Vapor/Vapor. These drive the design-selection charts and the phase-behaviour profile further down; the base thermal calculation is Kern sensible/latent per the phase chosen.

Both counter-current and co-current are calculated for comparison automatically — you do not choose a flow direction here.

Step 2 — Process parameters

  1. Smart Input — pick the one quantity calculated from the energy balance Q = m·Cp·ΔT; the rest become your inputs. The auto field is greyed, tagged AUTO-CALCULATED, and filled in once you run the calculation.
  2. Flow Rate, Temperature Inlet/Outlet, Operating Pressure, Cp, k, Density, Viscosity, Viscosity at wall — entered per side (tube = cold column, shell = hot column, by convention). Leaving Operating Pressure at zero lets the software suggest a typical value for the fluid selected.

Step 3 — Tube geometry & shell configuration

  1. Tube OD, Tube ID — the tube catalogue size; OD is a fixed manufacturing selection, ID auto-fills from it and is editable for a non-catalogue wall thickness.
  2. Tube Length — pick a TEMA standard length or type a custom one; the practical road-transport limit is 7315 mm (24 ft), enforced later by the auto-fix tool.
  3. Tube Layout and Pitch Ratio (Pt/Do) — 30° triangular gives the best heat transfer per shell volume; 90° square trades some heat transfer for mechanical cleaning access. A wider pitch ratio spaces tubes further apart, easing shell-side flow at the cost of a larger shell.
  4. Baffle Cut and Number of Tube Passes (Np) — 25% cut is the TEMA-standard starting point; more passes raise tube-side velocity (and U) at the cost of pressure drop.
  5. Shell Passes — 1 for a plain E-shell; 2 or 3 models shells connected in series, each drawn and BOM'd separately in the manufacturing package.
  6. Baffle Spacing Ratio (B/Ds) — the lever that actually controls baffle spacing; the Baffle Spacing (B) field beside it is a read-only result (ratio × shell ID), so adjust the ratio, not the spacing directly.
  7. TEMA Model — the fast path: pick a standard combination (AES, BEU, AKU, and so on) and the Front-Head, Shell Type and Rear Head selects below are set for you. Choose CUSTOMIZE to pick the three independently — useful when your specification calls for a combination outside the seven common presets.
  8. Shell Casing Shape — cylindrical is the TEMA/ASME standard; square or rectangular duct shapes are offered for compact or waste-heat-recovery style casings and feed the 3D view and report.
  9. Shell ID (Ds) is a read-only result — the bundle diameter implied by tube count and layout, plus the TEMA clearance for the rear-head type chosen. It is not an input you set directly.

Step 4 — Thermal design basis

  1. Tube Material — fills the wall conductivity (kw) and typical fouling resistances (Rdi/Rdo) for you; choose User Defined to enter your own.
  2. Inside/Outside Fouling (Rdi/Rdo) — overwrite the filled defaults whenever you have a service-specific fouling figure.
  3. U Assumed — the trial overall coefficient the design starts from; it auto-fills from the tube/shell fluid-pair reference band shown beside it the moment both fluids are selected, and is editable.

Step 5 — Run

Press RUN STHE CALCULATION. The results panel, the TEMA-driven 3D view and every chart populate together.

Reading the results

  • Cooling Water Warning — appears automatically whenever the tube-side fluid is water and its outlet exceeds 40°C, with the reason (scaling risk) and the mass flow that would bring it back to 40°C.
  • Key Results panel — every important output tagged AUTO-CALCULATED or USER INPUT, including heat duty, both mass flows, both outlet temperatures, tube count, bundle and shell diameter, LMTD, overall U, excess area, both-side ΔP and commercial nozzle sizes.
  • Design Tuning Assistant — appears whenever excess area falls outside the 10–40% band or the shell exceeds the 1524 mm (60") practical limit. Each row proposes one lever (tube length, passes, OD or baffle spacing) with an APPLY button, or press ⚡ AUTO-FIX TO TEMA to let the engine iterate every lever together — converging the assumed U onto the calculated U, stepping through TEMA standard lengths, and adding passes once the length limit is reached — until the design lands in-band or it runs out of levers.
  • Charts — temperature profile and U comparison for the operating point; a U₀ estimate nomograph by service-fluid category with your operating point marked; a shell−bundle clearance chart showing why the selected rear-head type was chosen; an Ft (LMTD correction factor) feasibility chart for the shell-pass/tube-pass combination, flagging a temperature cross; and a phase-change behaviour profile for the phases chosen in Step 1.
  • Flow Comparison — press FLOW COMPARISON to run the same duty as counter-current and concurrent side by side and see the LMTD and effectiveness penalty of a co-current arrangement.

Report & manufacturing package

VIEW STHE REPORT opens the technical datasheet — design data sheet, configuration, thermal performance, geometry, pressure drop and nozzle sizes, plus the design-selection nomographs, in whichever unit system is active. 2D MFG DRAWING + BOM opens the fabrication package: a general-arrangement drawing, separate front-head/shell/rear-head detail sheets with their own bills of material, a tube-sheet and pitch detail, the nozzle schedule, the combined BOM, fabrication & inspection notes and the auto-upgraded design points, all dimensioned in the active unit system.

Some manufacturing call-outs stay in millimetres. Small catalogue hardware — stud-bolt sizes, gasket and baffle-plate thicknesses, tie-rod diameters — are quoted in mm regardless of the active unit system, the same way a real fabrication drawing prints standard hardware in its catalogue size. Every functional dimension (tube length, shell ID, baffle spacing, tube OD/ID) converts with the rest of the report.
